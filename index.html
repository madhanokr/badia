Where does it hurt?

From your fingertip to your shoulder, pick the spot.

Finger

Fingers that catch, curl in or won’t straighten are usually tendons or the tissue around them. Most are fixed with small, quick procedures, often while you stay awake.

Common problems

  • Trigger finger
  • Mallet finger
  • Boutonnière deformity
  • Finger arthritis

How Dr. Badia treats it

  • Trigger finger releaseOften done awake
  • Mallet finger careSplint or repair
  • Joint procedures for finger arthritisSmall-joint care

Six hand surgeries in one day, a patient from the Dominican Republic

Thumb

Pain at the base of the thumb when you turn a key or open a jar is usually arthritis of the joint that lets the thumb swing across the palm. This is where Dr. Badia does some of his most distinctive work.

Common problems

  • Thumb arthritis (basal joint)
  • UCL injury (skier’s thumb)
  • Thumb tendon injuries
  • De Quervain’s tenosynovitis

How Dr. Badia treats it

  • BioPro thumb joint replacementKeeps the bone
  • Thumb arthroscopyCamera-guided
  • Thumb ligament repair (UCL, RCL)For unstable thumbs

Jordan Belfort after his thumb joint replacement

Hand

Numb fingers at night, a cord pulling a finger into the palm, a broken bone that healed crooked. Most hand surgery here happens in the office or at the surgery center in the same building.

Common problems

  • Carpal tunnel syndrome
  • Dupuytren’s contracture
  • Hand arthritis
  • Hand fractures
  • Rheumatoid arthritis

How Dr. Badia treats it

  • Endoscopic carpal tunnel releaseIn-office, awake
  • Dupuytren’s treatmentRelease of the cord
  • Fracture fixationSame-building surgery

An OB-GYN from Barbados back to work after endoscopic carpal tunnel release

Wrist

A “sprain” that still hurts months later is often a ligament or cartilage tear that an X-ray can’t show. The wrist is crowded and unforgiving, and it rewards a surgeon who looks inside it.

Common problems

  • TFCC tear
  • Wrist arthritis
  • Wrist ganglion cyst

How Dr. Badia treats it

  • Wrist arthroscopyCamera-guided repair
  • Scapholunate ligament reconstructionFor hidden tears
  • Total wrist replacement or fusionFor worn-out wrists

A professional tennis player who came after four failed procedures

Elbow

Elbow pain that lingers through rest and therapy is usually a worn tendon. A torn biceps at the elbow is the opposite: it needs to be repaired quickly.

Common problems

  • Tennis elbow
  • Golfer’s elbow
  • Cubital tunnel syndrome
  • Distal biceps tendon tear

How Dr. Badia treats it

  • Tenex, ultrasound-guidedNo open incision
  • Distal biceps reconstructionFor complete tears
  • PRP and orthobiologicsGuided injections

Why patients travel to Doral

Three things you won’t easily find closer to home.

Each of these is something Dr. Badia has published, taught or filmed. None of them is right for everyone. All of them are worth knowing about before you agree to surgery somewhere else.

One week after a BioPro thumb joint replacement

Thumb-base arthritis, without removing the bone.

The usual operation takes out the trapezium, a whole bone at the base of the thumb, and fills the gap with a tendon from your forearm. Dr. Badia is one of the few American surgeons who replaces the joint instead, using a two-piece implant fitted to your anatomy.

The thumb keeps its length and its look, and patients film their follow-ups from the first week.

WAVR: wide-awake surgery with virtual reality

Awake for surgery, somewhere else entirely.

For many hand and wrist procedures you don’t need general anesthesia. You eat lunch, take your usual medicines, numb only the hand, and put on a virtual reality headset while Dr. Badia works. You can talk to him during the procedure and eat again right after.

A visit, start to finish

One building, from the first look to the last stitch.

Dr. Badia opened the center in 2008 to bring every step of hand, wrist, elbow and shoulder care under one roof.

The Doral Pavilion, 3650 NW 82nd Avenue

  1. Arrive

    The center sits on the ground floor of the Doral Pavilion, near three major highways and minutes from Miami International Airport.

    3650 NW 82nd Ave, Suite 103

  2. See Dr. Badia

    Your consultation is with Dr. Badia himself. The whole team is bilingual, and a coordinator handles records and scheduling for patients from abroad.

  3. Imaging, same building

    Digital X-ray, MRI and fluoroscopy on site, plus ultrasound with augmented reality that lets Dr. Badia guide injections while watching the joint, not a monitor.

  4. Surgery, same building

    Dr. Badia operates only at The Surgery Center at Doral, in the same building. A specialty team, a smaller setting, and fewer infection risks than a large hospital.

    Suite 201, same address

  5. Therapy and follow-up

    Rehabilitation is on site. Patients who fly home keep seeing Dr. Badia by video, and he can join a call with your own therapist.

  6. Stay nearby

    A partner hotel is directly across the street, with preferred rates at four more. The international team helps you book.

About Dr. Badia

A surgeon who also teaches surgeons.

Trained at Cornell, NYU and Bellevue, then in hand surgery in Pittsburgh and across Europe. In Miami since 1995. He co-founded a surgical training lab where surgeons from around the world practice on anatomy before they operate on patients, and he still runs an international hand fellowship.

  1. 1995Co-founds the Miami Hand Center after fellowships in Germany, France, Switzerland and Italy
